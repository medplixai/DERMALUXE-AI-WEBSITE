Why Pimples Keep Coming Back (and What Actually Stops Them)

11 Sep 2026 · 6 min read · Medically reviewed by Dr. Nikhitha Priyanka, MD (DVL)

You buy the cream the medical shop recommended. It works for two weeks. Then the pimples are back, sometimes worse, and now there are dark marks too. If this sounds familiar, you are not doing anything wrong. Acne is a chronic condition with four causes working together, and most quick fixes hit only one of them. Here is what is really going on, and what a dermatologist does differently.

Key takeaways

  • Acne has four causes: excess oil, blocked pores, bacteria and inflammation. A cream that treats only one will always fail eventually.
  • Over-the-counter steroid combination creams clear pimples fast and then cause rebound acne, thinning and dark patches. Stop them.
  • Stopping treatment as soon as skin looks clear is the number one reason acne returns. Maintenance is part of the treatment.
  • In women, acne that flares before periods or comes with irregular cycles or facial hair needs a hormonal check-up.
  • A dermatologist grades your acne, prescribes the right retinoid or medicine, adds peels or MNRF for marks and scars, and gives you a 2-minute daily routine that prevents comebacks.

The 6 real reasons your acne returns

1. You treated one cause out of four

Every pimple starts the same way: oil glands produce too much sebum, dead skin cells block the pore, acne bacteria multiply inside, and the body responds with inflammation. Anti-bacterial face washes kill some bacteria but do nothing about the blocked pores. A drying gel reduces oil for a few days and the pore stays blocked. This is why the same pimples reappear in the same places, month after month.

2. The medical-shop cream was a steroid

Many creams sold over the counter for pimples or fairness are combinations of a steroid, an antibiotic and an antifungal. They shrink pimples in days, which is why people trust them. But steroids thin the skin, cause rebound acne that is worse than the original, and on Indian skin leave red or dark patches and visible fine blood vessels. Dermatologists across India see this damage every single day. If your cream works in 2 to 3 days and the acne roars back when you stop, check the label with a doctor.

3. Parlour facials, scrubs and home remedies

Facials with steam and squeezing push bacteria deeper and spread the infection. Scrubs and walnut face washes create micro-tears that inflame active acne. Lemon, toothpaste and baking soda burn the skin barrier, which makes oil glands work harder. None of these address why the pore blocked in the first place.

4. You stopped as soon as the skin looked clear

This is the most common reason we hear in Eluru. Acne medicines like retinoids take 8 to 12 weeks to work, and clear skin at week 12 means the treatment is working, not that the acne is gone. Stop then, and the pores start blocking again within a month. A dermatologist tapers you to a light maintenance routine instead of stopping cold.

5. Hormones and PCOS

In women, acne along the jawline and chin that flares 7 to 10 days before periods is usually hormonal. If it comes with irregular cycles, weight gain, hair thinning or facial hair, PCOS should be checked. Creams alone will not control this; a dermatologist may add hormonal treatment after simple blood tests, and the acne settles when the cause is treated.

6. Picking, squeezing and touching

Squeezing a pimple pushes its contents deeper, doubles the healing time and is the single biggest cause of pitted scars and dark marks. Phone screens, pillow covers, helmets and hair oil sliding onto the forehead do the rest. Small habits, big difference.

What a dermatologist does differently

First, we grade your acne: comedonal (blackheads and whiteheads), inflammatory (red pimples), nodular or cystic (deep, painful lumps). Each grade has a different treatment, and using a mild plan for severe acne wastes months. Then we build a plan that hits all four causes at once:

  • A prescription retinoid at night to unblock pores and prevent new ones forming — the backbone of every acne plan
  • Benzoyl peroxide or a prescription antibiotic for bacteria and inflammation, always for a fixed course, never open-ended
  • Oral medicines for moderate to severe or cystic acne, so scars never get the chance to form
  • Hormonal evaluation and treatment when the pattern suggests PCOS
  • Salicylic or other medical peels every 2 to 4 weeks to clear congestion and fade marks faster
  • MNRF, PICO laser or subcision later for pitted scars, once active acne is under control

Just as important, we tell you what to expect: mild dryness in the first weeks, visible improvement by week 6 to 8, and a maintenance plan from month 3 that takes two minutes a day.

The 2-minute routine that keeps acne away

  • Morning: gentle non-foaming cleanser, oil-free moisturiser, gel sunscreen SPF 30 or higher (sunscreen prevents dark marks, it does not cause pimples)
  • Night: same cleanser, then the prescribed retinoid or maintenance cream on dry skin
  • No scrubs, no steam, no squeezing, no new products from social media without asking
  • Change pillow covers twice a week; keep hair oil off the forehead; wipe the phone screen
  • Diet: high-sugar foods, milk-heavy diets and whey protein worsen acne in some people; a dermatologist will tell you if this applies to you

If you are already on a treatment from a previous doctor, do not stop it before your consultation. Bring the tubes with you; the label tells us a lot.

When to see a dermatologist without waiting

  • Painful, deep lumps or cysts — these scar within weeks
  • Acne that started or got worse after age 25
  • Dark marks or pits already appearing
  • Acne with irregular periods, hair thinning or facial hair
  • Acne on the chest, back or shoulders
  • Any cream that cleared the skin in days and then made it worse

Frequently asked questions

Can acne be cured permanently?

Acne can be brought under full control and kept clear with the right plan and a light maintenance routine. Hormonal acne may need longer-term care. What you can expect is clear skin, not lifelong pimples.

How long does dermatologist acne treatment take?

Most patients see clear improvement in 6 to 8 weeks and good control by 3 months. Marks fade over 3 to 6 months with peels; pitted scars are treated afterwards with MNRF or laser.

Is it safe to use a pimple cream from the medical shop?

Only if a dermatologist has checked it. Many over-the-counter pimple and fairness creams contain steroids that cause rebound acne and dark patches on Indian skin.

Does diet cause pimples?

Diet is not the main cause, but high-sugar foods, excess milk and whey protein can worsen acne in some people. Your dermatologist will tell you if a change is worth trying.
