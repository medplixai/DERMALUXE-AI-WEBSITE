Understanding psoriasis

In psoriasis the immune system speeds up skin cell turnover, so cells pile up as scaly plaques. It runs in families and is triggered by stress, infections, some medicines, smoking, alcohol and skin injury. It can also affect nails and joints (psoriatic arthritis), which is why a dermatologist should assess the whole picture — not just the patches. సోరియాసిస్ ఇమ్యూన్ సిస్టమ్ సమస్య, అంటువ్యాధి కాదు. ఒత్తిడి, ఇన్ఫెక్షన్లు, కొన్ని మందులతో పెరుగుతుంది. గోళ్ళు, కీళ్ళపై కూడా ప్రభావం ఉండొచ్చు.

What results to expect

Mild plaques clear in 3–6 weeks with topical therapy. Moderate to severe psoriasis improves steadily over 2–3 months on systemic treatment, with blood tests for safety. Frequently asked questions

Is psoriasis contagious?

No. It is an immune condition and cannot spread by touch, sharing clothes or contact.

Can psoriasis be cured permanently?

There is no permanent cure yet, but it can be controlled so well that the skin stays clear for long periods. Early, consistent treatment gives the best control.

Is it safe to take oral medicines for psoriasis?

Yes when prescribed and monitored by a dermatologist with periodic blood tests. Self-medication with steroids or unlabelled 'herbal' powders is what causes harm.

Does diet affect psoriasis?

Weight control, avoiding alcohol and smoking, and managing stress reduce flares. There is no single 'psoriasis diet', but your doctor will guide you.
