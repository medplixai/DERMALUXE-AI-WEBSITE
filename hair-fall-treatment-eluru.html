Finding the cause of your hair fall

Hair fall in men is usually genetic (androgenetic alopecia); in women it is often linked to iron or vitamin D deficiency, thyroid, PCOS, post-pregnancy changes, stress or crash dieting. Dandruff, fungal infection and alopecia areata cause patchy loss. Frequently asked questions

Is my hair fall normal or do I need treatment?

Shedding 50–100 strands a day is normal. Visible thinning, a widening parting, a receding hairline, bald patches or hair fall lasting more than 2–3 months should be examined by a dermatologist.

Can hair regrow after hair fall?

Yes, as long as the hair follicle is alive. Thinning areas respond well to medicines and PRP/GFC. Completely bald areas where follicles have closed need a hair transplant.

Is PRP or GFC better?

GFC is a more concentrated, purified version of PRP with a higher growth-factor yield and less discomfort. Your doctor will recommend the right option based on your scalp and budget.

Which doctor will treat my hair loss?

Hair fall is treated by our MD dermatologists; hair transplant procedures are performed by our hair transplant surgeons.
